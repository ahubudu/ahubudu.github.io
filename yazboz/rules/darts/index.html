How to play Darts: rules and scoring

501 / 301 / 701

Goal: Bring your score down to exactly zero before anyone else.

Setup

A standard board hangs with the bull’s eye 173 cm (5 ft 8 in) high; the throw line is 237 cm (7 ft 9¼ in) away. Everyone starts on 501; 301 is a shorter game and 701 a longer one. Closest to the bull throws first.

How to play

On your turn you throw three darts. The board has segments 1 to 20; the thin outer ring doubles the number, the thin inner ring triples it. The outer bull is 25, the bull’s eye 50 and counts as a double. What the three darts score comes off your total. The most in one turn is 180 (three triple 20s).

Scoring

A turn that would take you below 0, leave you on 1, or reach 0 without a double when playing double out is a bust: it doesn’t count at all and your score stays where it was. Reaching 0 with a double finishes the game (a checkout). The highest finish in one turn is 170 (T20, T20, bull).

Winning

First to exactly 0 wins the leg. Matches are played in legs: on setup you pick how many legs win the match (for example 3). Every leg starts again from 501, and the next player along throws first in each leg. Big tournaments group legs into sets as well.

Note

Variants: with double in, your score doesn’t come down until you hit a double, common in 301. With double out off, any dart that reaches 0 finishes, easier for beginners. Cricket (closing 15–20 and the bull) and Around the Clock (hitting 1 to 20 in order) are popular too. In partner play teammates throw in turn and share a score.

Cricket

Goal: Close 15 to 20 and the bull before anyone else, without being behind on points.

Setup

Same board and distance as 501. The only targets are 15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 20 and the bull; other numbers count for nothing. Write the seven targets on the board for every player.

How to play

On your turn you throw three darts. A single on a target is one mark, a double two, a triple three; the outer bull is one mark and the bull’s eye two. Three marks close that target for you (/, ✕, ⊗). Close the targets in any order you like.

Scoring

If you keep hitting a target you’ve closed while at least one opponent still has it open, every extra mark scores the number’s value (the bull 25). A target everyone has closed is dead and scores nothing more.

Winning

The first player to close every target and have at least as many points as everyone else wins. If you’ve closed everything but are behind, you keep scoring until you’re ahead or your opponents close too.

Note

Variants: in cut-throat cricket extra marks score for your opponents and the lowest score wins; the app uses the standard rules for now. Some tables add 14, 13, or the treble and double rings as targets. In partner play teammates share their marks.
