How to play Billiards: rules and scoring

Pool (8, 9 or 10-ball), three-cushion or snooker: pick the variant in the rules. Pool is played in racks, three-cushion to a target, snooker in frames.

8-ball / 9-ball / 10-ball

Goal: Pocket the balls by the rules to win each rack, and be first to the agreed number of racks.

Setup

A standard table has six pockets. For 8-ball, 15 balls go in the triangle with the 8 in the middle; for 9-ball, balls 1 to 9 in a diamond with the 1 at the front and the 9 in the middle; for 10-ball, ten balls in a triangle with the 10 in the middle. Toss a coin or lag to decide who breaks.

How to play

In 8-ball, after the break the first player to pocket a solid (1–7) or a stripe (9–15) takes that group. Once your group is cleared you pocket the 8, calling the pocket. In 9-ball and 10-ball the cue ball must first hit the lowest-numbered ball on the table on every shot; any ball that then drops counts. 10-ball is played with called shots. On a foul (scratching the cue ball, hitting the wrong ball first, no ball reaching a cushion) the opponent gets ball in hand anywhere on the table.

Scoring

In 8-ball you win the rack by clearing your group and pocketing the 8 in the called pocket; pocketing the 8 early, or with the cue ball, loses it. In 9-ball and 10-ball you win by legally pocketing the 9 or the 10; in 9-ball the 9 on the break wins too (a “golden break”). Each rack is one point.

Winning

Matches are races: the first to win the agreed number of racks (for example race to 5) takes the match. The break can alternate or go to the winner; agree on it at the start.

Note

Variants: in doubles, partners take turns. Some tables call only the 8, others every ball. In bars the loser sits down and the winner stays on. You can mark a break and run in the app.

Three-cushion

Goal: Make legal three-cushion caroms and be first to the target.

Setup

A table with no pockets and three balls: two cue balls (white and yellow, one per player) and a red. The break is decided by a coin toss or a lag; the balls’ starting spots are marked on the cloth.

How to play

You strike your own ball. It must touch both other balls, and hit cushions at least three times before it touches the second ball; the cushions can be the same or different. You keep shooting as long as you score; the first miss ends the inning and it’s the opponent’s turn.

Scoring

Every legal carom is a point. Write down the points of each inning. The average, total points divided by innings, shows a player’s level; the highest run is worth noting too.

Winning

First to the target (for example 25, 30 or 40) wins. With equal innings, if the player who broke gets there, the opponent still plays their last inning; if they reach it as well, the game is a tie or goes to extra innings.

Note

Variants: straight rail (free game) has no cushion rule; in one-cushion the cue ball must hit a cushion at least once before the second ball. Tournaments in Turkey are usually played to 30 or 40 with a shot clock.

Snooker

Goal: Pot the balls in the right order and finish the frame with more points than your opponent.

Setup

On the large table, 15 reds go in the triangle and six colors on their spots: yellow 2, green 3, brown 4, blue 5, pink 6, black 7. The white is the cue ball.

How to play

On your turn you must first pot a red, then any color, then a red again, and so on. Potted colors go back on their spots while reds remain. When the reds are gone the colors are potted in order, yellow to black. When you miss, it’s your opponent’s turn. The points of one visit are a break.

Scoring

Reds are 1, colors 2 to 7. A foul (hitting the wrong ball first, potting the white, missing every ball) gives the opponent at least 4, or the value of the ball involved if that’s higher. The highest possible break is 147.

Winning

The frame ends when the last black goes down; the higher score takes it. Matches are best of: in a best of 5, the first to 3 frames wins. If the scores are level, the black is re-spotted and played.

Note

Variants: shorter games use 6 or 10 reds. In doubles, partners take turns. In the app you enter the points at the end of each frame and, if you like, the highest break.
