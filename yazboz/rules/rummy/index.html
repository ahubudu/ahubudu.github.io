How to play Rummy: rules and scoring

Goal: Lay all your tiles in runs and groups before anyone else, and score the most over several games.

Setup

106 tiles: two sets of 1 to 13 in four colors, and 2 jokers. Everyone takes 14 tiles; the rest stay face down in the middle (the pool). With 5–6 players use the 160-tile set.

How to play

A run is three or more consecutive numbers in one color (red 4-5-6); a group is three or four of the same number in different colors. Your first meld must add up to at least 30, from your own tiles only. After that you may split and rearrange the sets on the table as you like, as long as only valid sets are left at the end of your turn. If you can’t lay anything, draw a tile from the pool and your turn ends. A joker stands for any tile; you can take a joker from the table by putting the right tile in its place, and must use it that same turn.

Scoring

A game ends when someone lays their last tile and calls “Rummy!”. Everyone else adds up the numbers on their rack and scores it as a minus; each joker left counts 30. The winner scores the total of all of it. If the pool runs out and nobody can go out, the lowest rack wins: the others lose the difference between their total and the winner’s, and the winner scores the sum of those differences.

Winning

The table agrees on how many games to play; in the official rules a round is one game per player (four games with four players). After the last game, the highest total wins.

Note

Variants: some tables play to a target score or count a joker as 50. Okey and 101 Okey are Turkish members of the same family, with an indicator tile and a wild okey. You can turn on “double for going out in one turn” under house rules.

House rules

The rules tables argue about most. Agree on them before you start; you can switch them on or off when you set up the game.

Double for going out in one turn

A player who lays all their tiles in a single turn, without having laid any before, scores double for that game, and everyone else loses double.
